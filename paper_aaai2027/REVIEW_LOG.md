# DISTIL — review & revision log

This file records every round of the reviewer-assembly loop: each reviewer's critique,
the author agent's point-by-point response, and the reviewers' acknowledgments in the
following round. Draft snapshots for each round live in `drafts/round_*/` (LaTeX + PDF +
CHANGES.md); the accepted version is copied to `drafts/final/`.

Reviewers:
- **R1 — Method & soundness**: math, algorithm, mechanism-vs-claims, logical validity.
- **R2 — Experiments & evidence**: protocol, baselines fairness, numbers, figures/tables, overclaiming.
- **R3 — Presentation & style**: clarity, flow, AAAI compliance, human (non-AI) prose.

## Round 1

### R1 — Method & soundness (score 5.5, not satisfied)

> The paper attacks a real and well-motivated gap — under a fixed demonstration budget, when-to-query gates ignore which failures to correct and where to place corrections — and the method is clearly described, the numbers match the reported protocol, and the writing is unusually disciplined. My concerns are with soundness of the arguments and precision of mechanism descriptions. (1) The central Q2 argument is a false dichotomy: high pre-finetune loss is claimed to imply either an underrepresented region or a bad demonstration, with the latter "ruled out by construction." A third case is omitted — represented but hard-to-fit regions (multimodal expert actions, aleatoric noise, stochastic denoising-loss estimates) — so the claim that the metric "measures exactly the diversity" does not follow; and the follow-on sentence that high gain is "necessary but not sufficient" is contradicted by the paper's own tables (Diff-DAgger has middling gain yet best-baseline SR on Push-T). (2) As written, SafeDAgger and DropoutDAgger score against the expert's action at every visited step, which deviates from the published algorithms and grants baselines uncounted expert access; the implementation and budget accounting must be stated. (3) The flagging rule (per-episode 95th-percentile threshold) flags steps in every episode and cannot define the failure set F_r. (4) The cluster-memory kernel is ill-defined across rounds for image runs (per-round standardization and refit PCA of varying dimension), and lambda=1 mixes loss units with kernel units. (5) The online/offline-bridge contribution overstates: one prescription is standing per pause, yet the text promises recording "the prescribed demonstrations in one batch days later." (6) Eq. 9 presents an offset-cap-from-representative mechanism that, per the implementation, is an absolute clamp to the native reset range on Lift/Door. (7) The intro's "rarer modes starve" critique is in tension with Eq. 8's restriction to near-dominant clusters. Each of these is fixable by precise restatement rather than new experiments, and several minor notation issues (binary vs. categorical cross-entropy, undefined K and a^nov, Stagger not fitting the "every baseline is a per-state predicate" template, "no budget is ever wasted") should be cleaned up. I would raise my score substantially if the arguments are restated at their supportable strength.

### R2 — Experiments & evidence (score 5, not satisfied)

> The paper attacks a real and under-studied question (which failures to correct and where to place demonstrations under a fixed budget), the experimental grid is broad (5 tasks x 2 modalities x 6 baselines), and every number I checked in the abstract and results text matches the tables exactly, including the +5.4, +25.7, +6.4 and +2.5 point margins and the r=0.82-0.89 range. The authors also hedge honestly about overlapping seed spreads in the tight cells, which I appreciate.
>
> However, the evidence chain has real gaps. First, the justification for the baseline-roster asymmetry is internally inconsistent: Section "Baseline Query Rules" claims Stagger cannot run on the robot tasks because visited states cannot be re-instantiated as demonstration starts, yet DISTIL's own targeted arm "re-instantiates the scene" of a recorded failure and replays to t* on those very tasks. A uniform-random control over recorded failures is therefore implementable exactly where DISTIL's headline wins are, and its absence weakens the causal story that clustering/memory (not just failure-replay correction) drives the gains. Second, Table 1's caption says mean±std over 5 seeds, but several DISTIL cells are arithmetically impossible under that definition (Door image 99.2±3.4 and Door state 98.4±4.2 exceed the maximum sample std achievable by five values bounded at 100), and DISTIL's std equals Diff-DAgger's in three cells; the ± definition must be clarified or the values recomputed. Third, no per-seed win counts or paired tests support the claimed separation on Push-T/Wipe/Door, where 1-sigma intervals still overlap (Wipe state 95.5±6.0 vs 90.4±6.0). Fourth, key protocol details are missing: |D_0|, rollouts per round, held-out eval set size and composition, retrain-vs-finetune, several baseline thresholds, and what happens when a gate never fires. Fifth, the VLM/LLM stack is never named in the text. Smaller points: "dominates throughout training" overstates Figure 3 (curves are entangled early and mid-budget on Wipe), the confidence scatter shows n=152 of an expected 180 rounds without an inclusion rule, and "case (b) is ruled out by construction" is too strong given a PPO expert. All are fixable, but the roster asymmetry and Table 1 statistics must be addressed before I can support acceptance.
