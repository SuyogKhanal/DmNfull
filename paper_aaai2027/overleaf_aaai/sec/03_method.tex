\section{Problem Formulation}

The policy is any function $f_\theta$ mapping an observation to an action that
exposes a per-step loss at a visited state-action pair,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:signal}
\ell^{(i)}_t \;=\; \mathcal{L}\big(f_\theta,\; s^{(i)}_t,\; a^{(i)}_t\big),
\end{equation}
%
where $i$ indexes an episode and $a^{(i)}_t$ is the action the policy itself
executed. The requirement stops there. For a diffusion policy $\mathcal{L}$ is the
denoising loss \cite{chi2023diffusionpolicy}; for a discrete policy it is the
negative log-likelihood of the action.

The policy is fitted to an initial set $\mathcal{D}_0$ by behaviour cloning
\cite{pomerleau1988alvinn,bain1995cloning}. At the start of round $r$ it is rolled
out on a fresh pool drawn from the task's reset distribution, and those episodes
it fails form the round's failure set,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:failures}
\mathcal{F}_r = \big\{\, f_i = (\tau_i,\; \ell^{(i)}_{1:T_i}) \;:\; \tau_i
\text{ is a failed rollout of } f_\theta \,\big\},
\end{equation}
%
with $N = |\mathcal{F}_r|$, which varies by round, and $T_i$ the length of
trajectory $\tau_i$. The
round closes by acquiring $D$ demonstrations from the expert $\pi^\star$ and
aggregating them \cite{ross2011dagger},
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loop}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{D}_r &= \mathcal{D}_{r-1} \cup \{d_{r,1},\dots,d_{r,D}\},\\
\theta &\leftarrow \arg\min_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim\mathcal{D}_r}
\big[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}(f_\theta, s, a)\big] \ \text{ for } r \equiv 0
\!\!\pmod m,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
halting when $|\mathcal{D}_r| - |\mathcal{D}_0| = B$, so the loop runs $B/D$
rounds. Retraining runs at a per-task cadence $m$ set identically for every arm.
Success is read on a frozen held-out set that every arm shares and no arm sees
during acquisition.

Every gated method compared here instantiates the same three-part acquisition rule
$A = (t^\star, C_{\mathrm{tgt}}, \xi)$, where $t^\star$ is the step at which the
expert takes over (when), $C_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ the failure mode the round is spent
on (which), and $\xi$ the state from which the corrective demonstration begins
(where). The query gates compute $t^\star$ and inherit the other two from the
rollout that tripped the threshold; the uniform-random control fixes all three by
sampling. DISEIL adopts the same $t^\star$ and computes the remaining two,
changing nothing else in the loop, so the comparison is controlled.

\section{DISEIL}

DISEIL runs four stages once per round (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}): perceive
the round's failures, partition them into failure modes, prioritise one mode, and
prescribe the $D$ demonstrations the expert is asked to supply. Two models fill
three roles. The \emph{vision-language model} \cite{bai2025qwen3vl} reads rendered
frames. The \emph{prescription model} ($\mathrm{PM}$) \cite{yang2025qwen3} is a
text-only reasoning model that labels each failure's root cause and prescribes the
round's request; neither enters the partition.

\paragraph{Perceive.} A round opens by describing each failure twice from the same
step of the same episode: geometrically, for the partition, and in language, for
the prescription. The two never mix. Each failure is anchored at the first step
whose loss crosses an out-of-distribution threshold $\eta$ and stays across it for
$K$ consecutive steps,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:flag}
t^\star_i = \min\big\{\, t : \ell^{(i)}_u > \eta \ \ \forall u \in [\,t,\,
t+K-1\,] \,\big\},
\end{equation}
%
falling back to $\arg\max_t \ell^{(i)}_t$ if no crossing occurs. The threshold is
a quantile of the training-loss distribution, recalibrated at every retrain; the
construction is Diff-DAgger's, used unchanged \cite{lee2025diffdagger}. The peak
arrives late, so an expert taking over there inherits a corrupted state with
little episode left.

Each failure is then reduced to a six-dimensional vector computed from the
privileged simulator state at $t^\star_i$, on the manipulation tasks
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:desc}
\begin{aligned}
\phi_i &= \big[\, p_{x,i},\; p_{y,i},\; \sin\psi_i,\; \cos\psi_i,\; \rho_i,\;
\delta_i \,\big],\\
\rho_i &= t^\star_i / T_i, \qquad
\delta_i = \big\| p^{\mathrm{ee}}_i - p^{\mathrm{obj}}_i \big\|_2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
where $(p_{x,i}, p_{y,i})$ is the planar position of the task-relevant object and
$\psi_i$ its yaw, entered through its sine and cosine so the wrap at $\pm\pi$ does
not create a false distance, $\rho_i$ the fraction of the episode completed before
the flag, and $\delta_i$ the end-effector-to-object distance. The same six slots
are refilled per task. The descriptor is small because the failure sets are small:
over a few dozen points, weakly informative dimensions flatten the pairwise
distances the merge order depends on, which the supplementary measures.

In parallel, three rendered frames, at the episode's start, at $t^\star_i$ and at
its end, go to the vision-language model, which returns a short spatial account.
The prescription model converts it into a root cause and a trajectory phase drawn
from a closed taxonomy stored in $\mathcal{K}$ rather than invented. The division
of labour follows the evidence: such models name a cause competently when given
structured evidence \cite{duan2025aha,liu2023reflect} and reason unreliably about
metric geometry from pixels alone \cite{chen2024spatialvlm,fu2024blink}.

\paragraph{Partition.} The round's failures are partitioned by a generic
clustering step $\mathcal{C}$ over the standardised descriptors,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kstar}
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{X}_i &= \frac{\phi_i - \mu_\phi}{\sigma_\phi}, \qquad
\{C_1,\dots,C_{k^\star}\} = \mathcal{C}\big(\tilde{X},\, k^\star\big),\\
k^\star &= \arg\max_{k \in [2,\,k_{\max}]} \operatorname{sil}(k),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mu_\phi$ and $\sigma_\phi$ are the per-dimension mean and standard deviation
of the round's own descriptors $\{\phi_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$, and
$k_{\max} = \max(2, \min(6, N-1))$. The step is generic by design: agglomerative
clustering is the instantiation used here \cite{ward1963hierarchical}, k-means
would serve \cite{lloyd1982kmeans}, and $k^\star$ is chosen by the silhouette
criterion, used unmodified \cite{rousseeuw1987silhouette,pedregosa2011sklearn}, so
DISEIL claims a partition step and not its implementation. Each mode carries its
raw-pose centroid, its mean peak loss $\bar{L}_C$, the mean over its members of
$\mathrm{peak}_i = \max_t \ell^{(i)}_t$, and a representative $\mathrm{rep}(C)$,
the member nearest the cluster mean in standardised space; the dominant mode
$C^\star$ has the most members, ties broken by mean peak loss. The peak scores a
failure while the first crossing locates it. Below four remaining failures each
failure becomes a singleton and Equation~\ref{eq:target} reduces to selecting the
highest-peak-loss failure. A mode is named by the majority root cause among its
members, since a method that reports a failure in mode 2 has said nothing.

\paragraph{Prioritise.} This stage makes the first of the two decisions DISEIL
adds, the which; Prescribe below makes the where. The
round is spent on the mode of highest mean peak loss among those within one
member of the largest,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:target}
C_{\mathrm{tgt}} \;=\; \arg\max_{C \,:\, |C| \,\ge\, |C^\star| - 1} \
\bar{L}_C .
\end{equation}
%
The size constraint keeps the target inside the bulk of the round's failures, so
a mode that barely exists cannot capture the round's budget on the strength of
one badly failed episode. Because one demonstration rarely removes a mode
outright, a rule of this shape can return the same mode round after round. DISEIL
therefore has a cluster memory, a configurable task-specific component outside the
core rule, which discounts modes already corrected and is on in every run here.

The prescription model is shown at most $\kappa$ cited failures from the target
mode: the representative first, then the worst-loss failure, then farthest-point
selection \cite{eldar1997fps} to span the mode.

\paragraph{Prescribe.} The prescription model receives the target mode's anchor
geometry, the cited failures with their root-cause labels, and the task's
constraints, and returns the round's request with a confidence score and a
rationale. Each requested demonstration takes one of two forms. A \emph{targeted
correction} names one cited failure; that exact episode is re-instantiated and the
expert takes over at the flagged step. A \emph{bridging placement} names two or
three cited failures and asks for a new configuration between them, from which the
expert demonstrates a complete episode. Bridging lets a prescription be easier than any failure it addresses: where a mode
lies far outside what the policy can solve, a targeted correction is a large
distributional jump and a bridged one is a step the policy can absorb. The
graded-placement argument is reverse curriculum generation's
\cite{florensa2017reversecurriculum}, applied to a demonstration request rather
than a training reset.

A model asked for a configuration of the world will sometimes name one the world
cannot produce. The graph makes such a request checkable, holding workspace
bounds, spawn ranges, reachability, controller limits, the success predicate and
the failure-mode vocabulary as structured knowledge, in the manner of a robot
knowledge base \cite{tenorth2013knowrob} rather than a retrieval index over text
\cite{lewis2020rag,edge2024graphrag}. Verification is a loop: the model proposes,
a map $g$ turns the proposal into a reset specification, the specification is
checked, and a violation is returned as feedback so the model can propose again,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:verify}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{cmd}^{(j)} &= \mathrm{PM}\big(C_{\mathrm{tgt}},\ S,\ \mathcal{K},\
\text{violation}(\xi^{(j-1)})\big),\\[1pt]
\xi^{(j)} &= g\big(\mathrm{cmd}^{(j)}\big),\\[1pt]
V(\xi) &= \mathbf{1}\big[\xi \in \mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{K}}\big] \wedge
\mathbf{1}\big[\mathrm{reachable}_{\mathcal{K}}(\xi)\big]\\[1pt]
&\quad \wedge\ \mathbf{1}\big[\mathrm{valid\text{-}path}_{\mathcal{K}}(\xi)
\big],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{K}}$ is the workspace bounds, the conjuncts of $V$
are the constraints $\mathcal{K}$ stores for that task, and
$\text{violation}(\xi^{(0)}) = \emptyset$ on the first attempt; on the grid task
the last conjunct is a path-validity predicate decided by A\textsuperscript{$\star$}
and breadth-first search \cite{hart1968astar,cormen2022algorithms}, which are
never the expert. A failed attempt consumes no budget, since the budget counts
demonstrations collected and not prescriptions proposed, and after $J_{\max}$
attempts the round falls back to the nearest untried recorded failure. A second
screen, which rejects a prescription the policy already solves, is specified in
the supplementary and is not exercised in any run below.

Every stage bears on what the policy is trained on and none on how it is fitted,
so the evaluation varies the acquisition rule and holds the rest of the loop
fixed. The supplementary states the round as pseudocode.
