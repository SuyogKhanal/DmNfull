Here are straightforward answers.

1) Where in the maze does the AI get confused?
- Right at the very start, in the tiny area around the starting square. There’s a dangerous tile sitting next to the starting position, so the first decision point is all about avoiding that hazard.

2) What should a good walkthrough look like in that area?
- The demo should first point out that the neighbor on the hazard’s side is dangerous and should not be chosen.
- Then it should show choosing a safe first move that moves away from the danger, and following a safe route along a clear corridor to keep away from hazards.
- It’s helpful to show more than one safe corridor option (for example, using a safe path along the outer edge and another safe route that still avoids the danger).
- The human should explain briefly why this choice is safer than going toward the hazard.

3) What will the AI learn from seeing that walkthrough?
- It will learn that when a dangerous tile is right next to the start, the first move must avoid stepping into danger.
- It will learn to prefer safe corridors that skirt around hazards, instead of pushing straight toward the goal.
- It will get practice recognizing this pattern in different fire layouts, not just one fixed case, helping it generalize.

4) How many walkthroughs are needed and how should they vary?
- Do several demonstrations—about 4 to 6 is a good starter batch.
- Vary the near-start hazard placements so the AI sees different ways a danger can sit next to the start.
- Vary which safe corridor is used (left-edge, down-left, etc.) so the AI learns multiple robust safe routes, not just one fixed path.
- Keep the core idea the same: don’t move into a hazard on the first move, and use a safe corridor to reach the goal.
