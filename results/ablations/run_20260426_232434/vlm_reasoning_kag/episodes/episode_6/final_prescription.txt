Here are plain answers to your four questions, based on the tricky start area where a hazard sits right next to the starting square.

1) WHERE in the maze does the AI get confused?
- At the very beginning, when there is a danger tile directly next to where you start. The AI tends to push toward the goal and ends up stepping into that nearby danger instead of taking a safe first move along a clear path.

2) WHAT should a good walkthrough look like in that area?
- Show that the first move is guided by danger sooner than by hurry to reach the goal.
- Demonstrate taking a safe, simple path that stays away from the immediate hazard, typically choosing a route along a safe corridor or wall.
- Then show continuing along that safe path toward the goal without stepping on hazards, even if it means a longer route.
- Use variations that hug different safe edges (for example along a left wall or along a bottom edge) so the idea of “avoid the immediate danger first, then progress safely” is clear.

3) WHAT will the AI learn from seeing that walkthrough?
- It will learn to treat an immediately adjacent danger as the top priority: don’t step onto a hazard even if the goal seems nearby.
- It will learn to follow safe corridors or edges when hazards are near, and only switch to toward-the-goal moves once the path is clearly hazard-free.
- It gains a pattern for generalizing this safe-start behavior to different mazes where hazards appear near the start.

4) HOW MANY walkthroughs are needed and how should they vary?
- Do about 5 to 8 demonstrations.
- Vary where the adjacent hazard sits around the start (same idea of “hazard right next to you,” but on different sides or in slightly different neighborhoods).
- Vary the safe corridor chosen (left-edge, bottom-edge, etc.) and vary the goal location so the model sees that safe corridor navigation works across different layouts.
- Keep some demonstrations that emphasize staying on a safe path for a while before approaching the goal, so the model learns a reliable hazard-avoidance habit that generalizes to other random mazes.
