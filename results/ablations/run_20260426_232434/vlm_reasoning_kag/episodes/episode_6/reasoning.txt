  - Fire tile is directly adjacent and immediately terminates the episode with a large negative reward (-10). The correct action would have avoided the hazard and chosen a corridor that keeps progress toward the goal while not stepping on a fire.

3) WHY THE POLICY FAILED (relation to dynamic config, KAG, and model design)
- Core failure mode: fire_collision (fm_fire_collision)
  - The agent collided with a fire tile in this configuration, indicating a failure to generalize fire avoidance to this start/goal/fire arrangement.
- Dynamic randomization constraints (DynamicConfig)
  - randomize_start, randomize_goal, randomize_fire are True per episode.
  - Goal and fires are randomized per episode; the minimum start-goal Manhattan distance is enforced (>= 4), but fire placements vary.
  - This places the agent in many configurations where a fire can lie immediately adjacent to the start or along plausible corridors; the policy must generalize fire-avoidance across these variations.
- How KAG informs the failure
  - Fire hazards are numerous and can cluster near the start or along potential corridors.
  - Corridors exist to avoid fires (Left-edge, Top-edge, Right-column) but must be chosen correctly depending on start/goal relative positions and fire layout.
  - The observed failure suggests the policy did not reliably identify the safe corridor when a fire was immediately adjacent to the start (0,1) in this episode.
- Observational and architectural factors that may contribute
  - Observation components: state_vector (14D) includes goal position and a 3x3 local neighborhood, plus steps_remaining; image renders hazards visually.
  - Policy constraints push for stochastic DDPM inference (200 steps) with multi-frame horizons (obs_horizon=4, pred_horizon=3). Although stochasticity is intentional, it can still produce unsafe short-horizon choices if the local signal is weak or misinterpreted.
  - CellAlignedEncoder pools each grid cell independently (no cross-cell bleed). If the local hazard (fire at [0,2]) is not strongly encoded relative to a bias toward “progress toward goal,” the model can misprioritize immediate path length or misinterpret the local hazard.
- How the failure manifests here
  - Despite the fire being immediately to the right, the model chose an action that moved into the fire. This implies either:
    - Insufficient emphasis on immediate hazard cues in the local neighborhood within the observed state, or
    - Demonstrations insufficiently covered this edge-case (start-adjacent-fire) leading to poor generalization to this configuration.
- Summary of root cause
  - Inadequate generalization for immediate-start fire hazards under dynamic randomization. The policy’s learned priors favored a non-avoidant action (toward “progress toward the goal”) even when a threat is directly adjacent, suggesting a mismatch between demonstrated safe behaviors in the data and the diverse configurations encountered in deployment.

4) WHAT THE POLICY GOT WRONG (contrasts and misalignments)
- The policy treated the rightward move as acceptable despite an adjacent hazard, indicating:
  - Underweighting of immediate local fire signals in the 3x3 neighborhood.
  - Overweighting of distance-to-go heuristics (the desire to reduce Manhattan distance) given the randomization, which in this case encouraged stepping into a hazard.
  - Possible coverage gaps in corrective demonstrations for edge-case configurations where a fire sits directly next to the start.
- In terms of KAG signals
  - Fire hazards and their terminating effect are clear, but the agent failed to internalize that immediate hazards should be prioritized over forward progress when a hazard is directly adjacent.
  - Corridors that avoid fires exist (Left-edge, Top-edge, Right-column). The agent did not exhibit a preference for these corridors in this episode, suggesting the need for more hazard-focused demonstrations in edge-case layouts.

5) REGIONS / CONFIGURATIONS NEEDING DATA (where to collect more demos)
- Edge-case: Start has an immediately adjacent fire tile
  - Collect demonstrations where the start position is adjacent to a fire tile (especially to the right or left) and the correct action is to avoid that cell and select a safe corridor.
- Dense-fire near-start scenarios
  - Examples where several fires are placed near the start (including adjacent and near-adjacent) to test robust corridor selection.
- Corridor-conditioned demonstrations
  - Proactively demonstrate left-edge corridor, top-edge corridor, and right-column corridor from various starts near the center of the grid, including when goal positions require different corridor choices.
- Dynamic variation with same corridor
  - Demonstrations that show successful navigation along the same corridor across different fire placements elsewhere, to reinforce corridor-based generalization.
- Local vs. global signals balance
  - Data should emphasize how to interpret the local neighborhood (3x3 signals) when hazards are present versus how to plan a corridor that yields long-term progress without entering hazards.
- Additional data goals
  - Increase coverage of start-goal-fire configurations with min_manhattan_dist ≥ 4 to ensure the model learns robust patterns rather than overfitting to specific coordinates.
  - Include corrective demonstrations that not only avoid the fire but also explicitly demonstrate switching to a safe corridor when a hazard is adjacent.
